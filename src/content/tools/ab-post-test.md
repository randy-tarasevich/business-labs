## How I use it

**Social Media Marketing, analytics unit.** 15–20 minutes. Good after Engagement Rate Lab, once students are comfortable with rates.

1. **Predict:** "A bake sale tried two posts. Version A: 200 people saw it, 6 clicked. Version B: 200 saw it, 10 clicked. Which post should they use from now on?" Almost everyone says B.
2. **Test:** Load the bake sale. The tool says luck could explain it about 39 times in 100. Then pairs work the "Try it" questions.
3. **Explain:** Each pair writes one sentence starting "Before you pick a winner, ..." using a number from the tool.

**Optional hook (5 minutes):** have every student flip a coin 20 times and count heads. The class gets a spread of results even though every coin is the same. That's exactly what the dots on the chart are.

The tool runs 1,000 fake tests where both versions have the same click rate (the combined rate) and counts how many show a gap as big as the real one, in either direction. That count is a simulated p-value. The "5 in 100" cutoff is a common convention, not a law; say so if students ask.

## Answer notes

| Preset | A | B | A's rate | B's rate | Gap this big by luck |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bake sale: small test | 6 of 200 | 10 of 200 | 3.0% | 5.0% | about 39 in 100 |
| Photo vs. text | 20 of 1,000 | 45 of 1,000 | 2.0% | 4.5% | less than 1 in 100 |
| Tiny gap, huge test | 600 of 20,000 | 700 of 20,000 | 3.0% | 3.5% | less than 1 in 100 |

- Try it #2: at 800 viewers each (24 vs. 40 clicks), about 3 in 100. At 400 each it's about 19 in 100; at 600, about 8 in 100.
- Try it #4: with identical results the gap is 0, so every fake test counts as "at least as big." The chart still shows a spread: that's normal random variation.
- The results are seeded, so the same inputs always give the same numbers. A different input gives a fresh set of fake tests.

## Common misconceptions

- **"B got more clicks, so B is better."** Small tests bounce around. The bake sale gap shows up by luck alone more than a third of the time.
- **"If it's real, it must be important."** A huge test can prove a tiny difference. Real and worth acting on are separate questions.
- **"Luck evens out, so a small test is fine."** Luck evens out only as the test gets bigger. That's the whole reason to test with more people.
- **"Less than 5 in 100 means it's 95% certain B is better."** It means a gap this big would be rare if the posts were the same. Close enough for class, but worth knowing the wording is careful for a reason.
