## How I use it

A 20–25 minute **predict, test, explain** activity after introducing A = L + E:

1. **Predict:** Pairs read each transaction and enter the change for every account that moves before clicking Check. The live line under the entry tells them if their entry balances, but balanced is not the same as correct.
2. **Test:** Check the entry. A wrong account turns red with a hint. Students fix it, or use Show answer.
3. **Explain:** After each post, one partner says out loud why equity did or did not change. Then work the "Try it" questions.

For whole-class use, project it in embed mode, take a show of hands on each account, then click Show answer.

## Answer notes

Final balance sheet after all 12 transactions:

| Account | Balance |
| --- | --- |
| Cash | $4,600 |
| Accounts Receivable | $0 |
| Supplies | $400 |
| Equipment | $20,000 |
| **Total assets** | **$25,000** |
| Accounts Payable | $300 |
| Loan Payable | $14,000 |
| Owner's Equity | $10,700 |
| **Liabilities + Equity** | **$25,000** |

Owner's equity: $10,000 invested + $2,100 revenue − $700 expenses − $700 withdrawal = $10,700.

## Common misconceptions

- **Buying equipment with cash makes the owner poorer** (transaction 3). It's an asset swap. Totals don't change.
- **A loan is income** (transaction 2). It's a liability. Equity doesn't move.
- **Paying a bill is an expense** (transaction 8). The cost was already recorded; paying it just shrinks cash and accounts payable.
- **Collecting from a customer is revenue** (transaction 9). The revenue was earned in transaction 6.
- **Owner withdrawals are expenses** (transaction 12). They reduce equity, but they aren't a cost of running the business.

Owner's Equity is shown as one account to keep the focus on the equation. In a full accounting course, split it into Capital, Revenue, Expenses, and Drawing.
