## How I use it

**Accounting 1, right after the T-Account Lab.** 30–40 minutes. Same food truck, one month later. Maya's capital opens at $10,700, the equity she ended month 1 with in the Accounting Equation and T-Account Labs, so students can see the story continue.

1. **Predict:** Before each adjustment, students say which kind it is (prepaid expense, unearned revenue, accrued expense, accrued revenue, or depreciation) and work out the amount on paper. The amount is the real skill here; the accounts follow from the kind.
2. **Test:** Enter the entry and Check. Wrong amounts get a nudge on the first miss and the full calculation on the second. Common mistakes get their own hint (see below). Any entry that uses Cash gets stopped with the rule: adjusting entries never use Cash.
3. **Explain:** Watch the net income panel as you go. After the last one, pairs work the Try it questions. #2 is the big idea: skipping adjustments made Maya's profit look $780 better than it was.

Point at the worksheet after adjustment (c). Accumulated Depreciation already had $300 from month 1, and now it's $600: it keeps growing, while Equipment stays at $20,000.

## Common misconceptions

The tool has a targeted hint for each of these.

- **Recording what's left instead of what was used** (a). $450 is on the shelf; $650 was used. The adjustment is always for the part that changed.
- **Adjusting for the whole prepayment** (b, d). $1,200 of insurance covers six months; only one month is used. $900 covers three events; only one is done.
- **Crediting Equipment for depreciation** (c). Accumulated Depreciation keeps the original cost visible on the balance sheet. Students also forget to subtract salvage, or use a full year instead of a month.
- **Using Cash in an adjusting entry.** If cash had moved, it would already be a regular transaction. Adjustments only fix the timing of revenue and expenses.
- **A full year of interest** (f), or adding interest to the loan itself. Interest owed goes in Interest Payable.
- **Crediting Unearned Revenue for work done before payment** (g). Unearned is for cash received *before* the work. Here the work came first, so it's a receivable.
- **"An accrued expense is next month's expense because that's when you pay it."** Try it #4: the cost belongs to the month the work happened. Paying it later just clears the liability.
