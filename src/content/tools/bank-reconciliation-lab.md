## How I use it

**Accounting 1, after the Closing Entries Lab.** 25–35 minutes. Same food truck, now at the end of month 3. The bank says $6,087 and Maya's Cash account says $6,240. Neither is right.

1. **Predict:** Before item 1, ask the class which number they'd trust and why. Then give pairs one rule to test as they go: "If Maya knew about it first, fix the bank's side. If the bank knew first, fix Maya's side."
2. **Test:** Students place each item and Check. The first miss gets a hint aimed at the mistake; the second names where it goes. Watch the gap close in the reconciliation panel. The journal entries table only fills up for items on Maya's side.
3. **Explain:** After the last item, pairs work the Try it questions. #1 (why outstanding checks need no entry) is the big idea. #2 is a good exit ticket.

Item 4 is a check that cleared both sides. It's there on purpose: students learn that matching items drop out of the reconciliation. Item 8 is a transposition error ($96 vs. $69). Point out that the $27 difference divides evenly by 9, a classic way to spot swapped digits.

This also works in Personal Finance as a stretch: the bank side is exactly what students do when they balance a checking account.

## Answer notes

| Item | What it is | Where it goes | Amount | Journal entry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deposit in transit | Add to bank | $650 | None |
| 2 | Outstanding check #218 | Subtract from bank | $180 | None |
| 3 | Bank service fee | Subtract from Maya's | $15 | Dr Bank Service Charge Expense, Cr Cash |
| 4 | Check #215, on both | No adjustment | | None |
| 5 | NSF (bounced) check | Subtract from Maya's | $120 | Dr Accounts Receivable, Cr Cash |
| 6 | Outstanding check #221 | Subtract from bank | $420 | None |
| 7 | Interest earned | Add to Maya's | $5 | Dr Cash, Cr Interest Revenue |
| 8 | Recording error ($96 recorded, $69 paid) | Add to Maya's | $27 | Dr Cash, Cr Fuel and Permits Expense |

Bank: $6,087 + $650 − $180 − $420 = **$6,137**.
Maya's Cash account: $6,240 − $15 − $120 + $5 + $27 = **$6,137**.

Starting gap: $153. Check: bank-side items net to +$50 and Maya's-side items net to −$103, which closes the $153 gap.

Some textbooks use Miscellaneous Expense for the service fee. Either is fine; the tool uses Bank Service Charge Expense.

## Common misconceptions

The tool has a targeted hint for each of these.

- **Subtracting outstanding checks from Maya's balance.** She already subtracted them when she wrote the checks. Doing it again counts them twice.
- **Adding the deposit in transit to Maya's balance.** Same idea: she recorded it when she made the deposit.
- **Putting the service fee or interest on the bank's side.** The bank already recorded both. That's how they ended up on the statement.
- **Adding a bounced check.** The money Maya counted never arrived, so her balance is too high.
- **Fixing the error by the full $96 or $69.** Only the $27 difference was wrong. And it's an add: she took out too much.
- **"Every item needs a journal entry."** Only Maya's side does. The bank side fixes itself when the deposit lands and the checks clear.
- **Adjusting for a check on both statements.** Item 4 is on both, so it explains none of the difference.
