## How I use it

A 10–15 minute **predict, test, explain** activity:

1. **Predict:** Before touching anything, students guess how many lemonade cups the stand has to sell to break even.
2. **Test:** Load the preset and check. Then work the "Try it" questions in pairs.
3. **Explain:** Each pair writes one sentence using *contribution margin* to explain why a $1 price change moves break-even so much.

## Answer notes

- **Lemonade stand:** $150 ÷ ($2.00 − $0.50) = 100 cups. At $3.00 it drops to 60 cups.
- **Price = variable cost:** contribution margin is $0, so the lines run parallel and never cross.
- **Food truck:** cutting rent by $1,000 → $5,000 ÷ $7 ≈ 715 meals. Cutting variable cost by $1 → $6,000 ÷ $8 = 750 meals. Here the rent cut wins.

## Predict first

Two prediction cards sit above the challenges. Students pick Goes up, Goes down, or Stays the same; the tool makes the change on screen and shows before and after. Works well projected: take a show of hands, click the class favorite, then discuss.

- **Forecast doubles** (T-shirt shop, 120 to 240 shirts): break-even **stays the same** at 100. Break-even depends on price, variable cost, and fixed costs, not on how many you expect to sell. The forecast only moves the dashed line and the profit. Follow-up: so what *does* change? (Profit, from $240 to $1,680.)
- **Raise price, sell less** (food truck, $12 to $13, 950 to 850 meals): profit **goes up**, $650 to $800. Each meal now contributes $8 instead of $7, which more than makes up for 100 lost sales. Follow-up: how many meals could it lose before profit drops below the old $650? (At 831 meals or fewer: 6,650 ÷ 8 = 831.25.)

## Challenges

Three challenge cards sit under the tool. Each Start button loads its scenario and makes a link you can post. Sample solutions (there are others):

- **Cut costs, not price** (T-shirt shop, break-even 75 or fewer, price $20 or less): cut variable cost to $4 (1,200 ÷ 16 = 75), or cut fixed costs to $900 (900 ÷ 12 = 75). Ask which is more realistic.
- **Price your way to profit** (phone case, $400 profit at 200 cases, price $32 or less, fixed costs stay $4,000): price $31 gives (31 − 9) × 200 − 4,000 = $400. A $6 price change swings profit by $1,200. Cutting variable cost also works.
- **Rent goes up** (food truck, fixed costs $7,500, break-even under 1,000): price $13 gives 7,500 ÷ 8 = 938 meals, or variable cost $4.50 gives 7,500 ÷ 7.50 = 1,000 (not under, so $4.40 or less). Good debrief: which lever would customers notice?

## Common misconceptions

- Students think raising the price always helps. Ask what happens to the forecast when customers have a cheaper option.
- Students forget to round break-even **up**. You can't sell 99.4 cups.
