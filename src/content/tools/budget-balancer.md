## How I use it

The digital version of the **Balance the Budget** scenario cards from *Budgeting on $2,400 a Month*. Same four people, same numbers as the printed cards.

- **In class:** pairs work one card, same steps as paper: label every line, find the gap, change amounts until it balances, write two trade-offs.
- **Absent students / homework:** the same task, done independently.
- **Debrief:** project Card A and Card C side by side. Same $2,400, very different needs percentages. Why?

Students click **Copy my budget** and paste the summary into Google Classroom or a Form. Nothing is saved or sent from the site.

## Differentiation

- **Card D** is the most accessible; **Card C** is hardest. Only the housing option balances Card C with real savings left over.
- **Extension (G/T):** change take-home pay to $1,900 and $2,900 to model Sofia's self-employed income swings. Which lines have to stay fixed in a $1,900 month?
- The 50/30/20 targets update automatically when take-home pay changes, so math gaps don't block the trade-off thinking.

## Answer notes (starting totals at $2,400)

| Card | Starts at | Over by | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| A · Marcus, Norwich | $2,685 | $285 | Needs start near 75%. The car is a need for his job. |
| B · Priya, New Haven | $2,705 | $305 | Three savings lines. Cutting eating out ($300) nearly balances it alone. |
| C · Sofia, New London | $2,546 | $146 | Rent alone is 61%. The shared house frees up $746. |
| D · Tyler, Old Lyme | $2,670 | $270 | Living at home makes the 50/30/20 split easy to hit. |

Labels are judgment calls on purpose (gym, phone upgrade, rideshare, Roth IRA vs. emergency fund). Grade the reasoning in the trade-offs, not a single right label.
