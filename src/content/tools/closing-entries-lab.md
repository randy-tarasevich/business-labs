## How I use it

**Accounting 1, after the Adjusting Entries Lab and the financial statements.** 25–35 minutes. Same food truck, same month: the tool starts from the adjusted trial balance students finished in the Adjusting Entries Lab ($32,230 on each side), so the story keeps going.

1. **Predict:** Step 1 is a sort. Before anyone clicks, have students list on paper which accounts they think start over next month. The sort is where the big misconceptions show up (see below), so let the class argue about Unearned Revenue and Accumulated Depreciation before checking.
2. **Test:** Students write each closing entry and Check. The first miss gets a hint aimed at the mistake; the second names the accounts and shows how to get the amount. Watch the two T-accounts on the right: Income Summary fills up with revenue and expenses, then empties into Capital.
3. **Explain:** After the post-closing trial balance, pairs work the Try it questions. #3 (why drawing doesn't lower profit) and #4 (what changes with a loss) are the ones worth discussing as a class.

Point at Income Summary after entry 2: its $2,570 credit balance is the same net income students found in the Adjusting Entries Lab. Closing doesn't change profit; it just moves it into Capital.

## Answer notes

**Step 1, the sort.** Close 8 temporary accounts: Sales Revenue, the six expense accounts, and Maya, Drawing. The other 12 are permanent and carry over: all five assets, Accumulated Depreciation, all five liabilities, and Maya, Capital.

| Entry | Debit | Credit | Amount | How to get it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Close revenue | Sales Revenue | Income Summary | $5,900 | $5,200 + $300 + $400 from adjustments (d) and (g) |
| 2. Close expenses | Income Summary | Each expense account | $3,330 | $650 + $200 + $300 + $1,440 + $90 + $650 |
| 3. Close Income Summary | Income Summary | Maya, Capital | $2,570 | $5,900 − $3,330, the net income |
| 4. Close drawing | Maya, Capital | Maya, Drawing | $800 | Drawing's balance |

Entry 2 is a compound entry. The tool lets students pick "All 6 expense accounts" as the credit; in the journal it's six credits: Supplies Expense $650, Insurance Expense $200, Depreciation Expense $300, Wages Expense $1,440, Interest Expense $90, Fuel and Permits Expense $650.

Maya, Capital: $10,700 + $2,570 − $800 = **$12,470**. That matches the ending equity on the statement of owner's equity.

## Common misconceptions

The tool has a targeted hint for each of these.

- **Closing Unearned Revenue** (sort). It has "revenue" in its name, but it's a liability: Maya still owes the school two catering events.
- **Closing Accumulated Depreciation** (sort). It grows over the truck's whole life. Students who think "it's like an expense" mix it up with Depreciation Expense, which does close.
- **Leaving Drawing open** (sort), or **closing Drawing through Income Summary** (entry 4). Drawing isn't an expense. It closes straight to Capital and never changes net income.
- **Closing revenue or expenses straight to Capital** (entries 1 and 2). It gives the right ending capital, but the four-step method most textbooks teach goes through Income Summary so net income shows up as one number.
- **Using unadjusted balances** ($5,200 of revenue or $1,850 of expenses). Closing always comes after adjusting.
- **Closing Income Summary on the wrong side** (entry 3). With a profit, Income Summary has a credit balance, so it gets debited. Try it #4 walks through the loss case, where it flips.
- **Using Cash.** No money moves when the books close.
