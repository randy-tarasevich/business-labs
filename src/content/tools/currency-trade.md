## How I use it

**International Business.** 20 minutes.

1. **Predict:** "If the dollar gets stronger, is that good or bad for an American company?" Take a vote. Most students say good.
2. **Test:** Load the olive oil (an import), then the guitars (an export), and slide the exchange rate in both. The bottom chart shows the whole range at once. Pairs work the "Try it" questions.
3. **Explain:** Students finish the sentence: "A stronger dollar helps ___ and hurts ___ because ___."

**Before class:** the exchange rates in the presets are made-up examples, not today's rates. A quick warm-up is having students look up today's real rate and type it in.

The tool sticks to the mechanics: how rates convert prices, who pays a tariff to the government, and how it reaches shoppers. That keeps discussion of current trade policy grounded in the numbers rather than opinions. Tariffs are figured on the product's cost, not shipping.

## Question levels

Rough Webb's Depth of Knowledge (DOK) levels for the student page, so you can pick questions to match your goal.

- **Try it 1:** DOK 2 (explain a cause or compare)
- **Try it 2:** DOK 2 (explain a cause or compare)
- **Try it 3:** DOK 2 (explain a cause or compare)
- **Try it 4:** DOK 1 (recall or find a number)
- **Decide and defend:** DOK 3 (no single answer; students take a position and back it with numbers). The student page lists what a strong answer includes, which doubles as a quick rubric.
- **Your turn:** DOK 3 (students write their own what-if question and test it). Good for an exit ticket: collect one question per pair and use the best ones as tomorrow's warm-up.

Students must type a few words before an answer unlocks. In embed mode the boxes are hidden and you can show any answer directly.

## Answer notes

- **Olive oil:** €6 ÷ 0.90 = $6.67. Landed cost $8.83 (with $0.67 tariff), shelf price $14.13 vs. $16.00 for U.S.-made. The tariff raises the shelf price by $1.07 after markup.
- **Sneakers:** 500,000 ₫ ÷ 25,000 = $20. Landed $26, shelf $52 vs. $60. The 20% tariff adds $4 at the border and $8 on the shelf. A 40% tariff makes them cost the same as the U.S.-made pair.
- **Guitars to Japan:** ($800 + $60) × 150 = ¥129,000, shelf ¥180,600 vs. ¥170,000 for a Japanese guitar. The dollar would have to weaken to about ¥141 per $1 for the U.S. guitar to match.
- **Kayaks to Canada:** shelf CA$1,745 vs. CA$1,700. At about CA$1.33 per $1 they'd match.

## Common misconceptions

- "Stronger dollar" sounds good for everyone. It helps importers and American shoppers, and hurts exporters.
- Students think the foreign country pays a tariff. The importing business pays it to its own government, then passes it on.
- Students read exchange rates backward. Anchor it: this tool always shows how much foreign money **one dollar** buys.
- Markups multiply a tariff: a $4 tariff became $8 on the shelf with a 100% markup.
