## How I use it

**Level: Advanced Accounting.** 15–20 minutes, after students have done one straight-line schedule by hand.

1. **Predict:** Students sketch what they think the book value line looks like for each method before loading the food truck.
2. **Test:** Compare their sketches to the chart, then work the "Try it" questions. The schedule tabs show every year in detail, in cents, so students can check homework.
3. **Explain:** Each pair writes one sentence on why a business might pick double-declining for taxes but straight-line for the report it shows the bank.

The tool calculates full years only (no partial first year). Double-declining never drops below salvage, and its final year is whatever brings book value down to salvage. If your textbook switches to straight-line partway through instead, say so; the totals still match.

## Answer notes

**Food truck** ($60,000 cost, $6,000 salvage, 8 years; $54,000 to depreciate):
- Straight-line: $6,750 every year.
- Double-declining: rate 25%. Year 1 $15,000, year 2 $11,250, year 3 $8,437.50. Year 8 is $2,009.03 to land on $6,000.
- Sum-of-years: digits total 36. Year 1 = $54,000 × 8/36 = $12,000; year 8 = $1,500.

**Laptop** ($1,500, $100 salvage, 3 years):
- Straight-line: $466.67 per year.
- Double-declining: $1,000, then $333.33, then $66.67.
- Sum-of-years: $700, $466.67, $233.33.

## Common misconceptions

- Students think depreciation is cash going out. The cash left when the asset was bought; depreciation just spreads the cost.
- Students depreciate the full cost. Salvage value is never depreciated.
- Students apply the double-declining rate to cost minus salvage. It applies to **book value**, and salvage only acts as a floor.
- Students think faster methods mean more total expense. Every method totals cost minus salvage.
