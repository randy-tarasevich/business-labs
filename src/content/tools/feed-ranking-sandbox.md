## How I use it

**Social Media Marketing, right after Engagement Rate Lab.** 15–20 minutes.

1. **Predict:** Read the 8 post titles out loud. Students rank the top 3 they think they'd see first on their own feed.
2. **Test:** Click through the four algorithms ("Newest first," "Most liked," "Keep them watching," "Friends first") and compare with their predictions. Then pairs work the "Try it" questions.
3. **Explain:** Each pair writes advice to the bakery owner: one change to their next post, and which number from the tool proves it helps.

The "Keep them watching" moment is the discussion. The shouting-match post wins because arguments get comments and long watch times. Ask: who benefits from a feed like that, and who pays for it? This is the ethics piece of the lesson.

Be clear that the model is invented. Real apps use many more signals and don't publish their formulas, but they have said publicly that signals like these (relationships, engagement, watch time, freshness) matter. The point is the idea of weighted signals, not any one app's recipe.

- Try it #3 (Keep them watching): raising only watch time to about 25 seconds gets the bakery to #3 (20 seconds gets #4). Raising only likes to 3,000, comments to 400, or shares to 300 doesn't get it past #5.
- Scores are relative: each signal is scaled against the busiest post in the feed, so changing your post can also change how the others score.
- "Reset my post" puts the bakery back to its starting numbers.

## Common misconceptions

- **"The feed shows posts in the order they were posted."** Only if newness is the only signal. Most feeds mix several.
- **"More likes is all that matters."** Under "Keep them watching," a post with 5,000 likes comes in third. What counts depends on what the app is trying to get you to do.
- **"The algorithm is neutral."** Someone chose the weights. Those choices decide what millions of people see first.
- **"A business just needs to post more."** Each post still has to earn its spot. Fitting what the feed rewards (here, watch time) matters more than volume.
