## How I use it

**Personal Finance, managing risk unit.** 15–20 minutes. Works well with students who are getting their license or about to be added to a family policy.

1. **Predict:** "Plan A has a $250 deductible for $230 a month. Plan B has a $1,000 deductible for $195 a month. Which would you pick?" Take a show of hands. Most pick Plan A because the deductible "feels safer."
2. **Test:** Project the tool. Load the quiet year (Plan B wins by $420), then one fender bender (Plan A wins by $330). Point at the break-even: Plan A only pays off with a claim about every 1.8 years. Pairs work the "Try it" questions.
3. **Explain:** Each pair writes when they'd choose the high deductible, and the one condition that has to be true first (enough savings to cover it).

The savings note is the second half of the lesson. A high deductible saves money over time, but only for someone who could pay it tomorrow. This is a natural tie-in to emergency funds.

## What the tool assumes

- **Example prices, not quotes.** The premiums ($230 and $195 a month) are made up to be in a believable range for a young driver; real prices depend on age, car, town, and driving record. Have students ask a parent what their policy costs, or look up the difference between deductibles on a real quote.
- Every claim in a year is the same size. You pay the repair up to the deductible; insurance pays the rest. Coverage limits aren't modeled.
- Filing a claim can raise your premium in later years. This tool doesn't model that, which makes small claims look a little better than they are. Worth mentioning: many people pay small repairs themselves.
- Break-even = (Plan A premium − Plan B premium) × 12 ÷ (what Plan A saves on each claim). When the repair is smaller than Plan B's deductible, Plan A saves less per claim, so the break-even rises.

## Answer notes

Plan A: $250 deductible, $230 a month. Plan B: $1,000 deductible, $195 a month. Savings $500.

| Preset | Claims | Repair each | Plan A year | Plan B year | Cheaper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet year | 0 | n/a | $2,760 | $2,340 | Plan B by $420 |
| One fender bender | 1 | $1,500 | $3,010 | $3,340 | Plan A by $330 |
| Rough year | 2 | $4,000 | $3,260 | $4,340 | Plan A by $1,080 |

- Break-even with repairs of $1,000 or more: $420 ÷ $750 = 0.56 claims a year, about one claim every 1.8 years.
- A $600 repair: Plan A saves only $350 on the claim, so break-even rises to 1.2 claims a year and Plan B wins that year by $70.

## Predict first

Two prediction cards sit above the challenges. Students pick Goes up, Goes down, or Stays the same; the tool makes the change on screen and shows before and after.

- **Bigger repair** (one fender bender, $1,500 to $3,000): Plan A's advantage **stays the same** at $330. Once the repair is bigger than both deductibles, insurance pays everything above them, so a bigger bill doesn't change what you pay. Follow-up: so what is insurance really for? (The big bills.)
- **Smaller repair** (one fender bender, $1,500 to $600): Plan B's year **goes down**, $3,340 to $2,940. Under the deductible, you pay the whole repair yourself. Follow-up: is it worth filing a claim for $600 on Plan B at all? (No; insurance pays nothing.)

## Challenges

Three challenge cards sit under the tool. Each Start button loads its scenario and makes a link you can post. Sample solutions (there are others):

- **How high can Plan B go?** (one $1,500 fender bender, premiums and Plan A locked): lower Plan B's deductible to $600 or less. At $650, Plan B wins by $20; at $700, Plan A still wins by $30. Point out the catch: a real insurer would raise Plan B's premium for a lower deductible.
- **A rough year** (two $4,000 accidents, deductibles locked): Plan B's premium has to drop to $100 a month or less ($3,200 vs. $3,260). Debrief: in a truly bad year, the low deductible wins easily.
- **Make the low deductible worth it** (break-even 0.25 claims a year or fewer, Plan B locked at $195): Plan A has to cost $210 a month or less (break-even 0.24). Ask whether a $15 a month difference for $750 less deductible is a deal worth looking for.

## Common misconceptions

- **"The low deductible is always safer, so it's better."** It costs more every month whether or not you crash. Over years with few claims, the high deductible usually wins.
- **"A bigger accident means I pay more."** Once a repair is bigger than your deductible, you pay the same amount no matter how big it gets.
- **"Insurance pays for everything."** You always pay up to the deductible first, and small repairs under the deductible are all on you.
- **"The cheaper plan is the right plan."** Only if you could cover the deductible tomorrow. Without savings, a high deductible can turn one accident into credit card debt.
