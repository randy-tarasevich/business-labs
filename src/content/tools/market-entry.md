## How I use it

**International Business, global expansion unit.** 20–25 minutes, after students know the five entry modes by name.

1. **Predict:** Before opening the tool, students rank the five entry modes from most to least profitable for a small market, then for a big one.
2. **Test:** Load the small test market, then the big growing market, and compare to their rankings. Then the big but risky market. Pairs work the "Try it" questions.
3. **Explain:** Each pair recommends an entry mode to a company's board in three sentences: one about profit, one about control, one about risk.

**Extension:** give groups a real company and a country. They set the year 1 sales and risks they think fit, defend their numbers, and present a recommendation. Every number in the "Five ways in" table can be changed.

**Simplifications to mention:** the upfront costs and profit shares are made-up but reasonable for a mid-size consumer brand. A tariff only hurts exporting, and costs the exporter the tariff rate times 0.4 (shipments are worth about 40% of what shoppers pay). "Political trouble" means owned operations (joint venture, subsidiary) earn nothing after year 2; the upfront money is already spent.

## Answer notes

| Market | Licensing | Exporting | Franchising | Joint venture | Own subsidiary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small test market ($300K) | $86,577 | **$199,784** | $116,522 | −$21,059 | −$142,117 |
| Big growing market ($3M) | $910,765 | $2,177,836 | $1,435,224 | $2,039,413 | **$3,978,825** |
| Big but risky market | $636,036 | $236,414 | **$995,657** | $301,250 | $502,500 |

- Subsidiary passes exporting at about **$730,000** in year 1 sales (with no risks turned on).
- In the risky market, each risk hurts a different mode: the 25% tariff cuts exporting to 2%, political trouble stops the joint venture and subsidiary after year 2, and the 30% sales miss hurts everyone. Franchising is the only option none of them hit hard.

## Common misconceptions

- Students assume owning everything is always best because you keep all the profit. The small market shows the upfront cost can sink it.
- Students ignore control. Licensing and exporting can make money while someone else decides how the brand is sold and priced.
- Students treat risk as "it might lose money." Talk about specific risks: tariffs, currency swings, political change, and a partner who disagrees.
- Joint ventures look like "half a subsidiary." They also add a partner who knows the local market, which is often the real reason to choose one.
