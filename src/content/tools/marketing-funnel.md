## How I use it

**Marketing, promotion unit.** 15–20 minutes.

1. **Predict:** Show only the bakery's inputs. Students guess how many customers $500 of ads will bring in. Most guess far too high.
2. **Test:** Load the preset and walk down the funnel together. Then pairs work the "Try it" questions.
3. **Explain:** Each pair writes a short "memo to the owner": keep running the ad, change it, or stop. They have to cite one number.

**Extension:** give each group a different lever (click rate, conversion rate, average order, cost per view) and a goal of $200 profit. Groups report what change their lever needed and whether it's realistic.

The funnel shape is drawn for readability, not to scale. Real drop-offs are steep: the bakery goes from 62,500 views to 30 customers.

## Question levels

Rough Webb's Depth of Knowledge (DOK) levels for the student page, so you can pick questions to match your goal.

- **Try it 1:** DOK 3 (reason with evidence and decide)
- **Try it 2:** DOK 1 (recall or find a number)
- **Try it 3:** DOK 3 (reason with evidence and decide)
- **Try it 4:** DOK 2 (explain a cause or compare)
- **Decide and defend:** DOK 3 (no single answer; students take a position and back it with numbers). The student page lists what a strong answer includes, which doubles as a quick rubric.
- **Your turn:** DOK 3 (students write their own what-if question and test it). Good for an exit ticket: collect one question per pair and use the best ones as tomorrow's warm-up.

Students must type a few words before an answer unlocks. In embed mode the boxes are hidden and you can show any answer directly.

## Answer notes

| Preset | Views | Clicks | Customers | Revenue | Profit after ads | Sales per $1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bakery | 62,500 | 750 | 30 | $840 | $4 | 1.68× |
| Tutoring | 20,000 | 800 | 24 | $7,200 | $4,240 | 9.00× |
| T-shirts | 200,000 | 1,600 | 32 | $800 | −$640 | 0.80× |

- T-shirt break-even conversion rate: about 5.6% (needs about 89 customers).
- The bakery's ads bring in $1.68 for every $1, yet only make $4. Revenue isn't profit: 40% of each sale goes to the product.

## Common misconceptions

- Students judge an ad by views. Views are cheap; customers are what matter. Compare cost per customer.
- Students think sales per $1 above 1.0 means profit. With a 60% margin, the bakery needs about 1.67× just to break even.
- Students assume doubling the budget doubles results. In practice, extra spending reaches less interested people.
- Conversion rate and click rate get mixed up. Click rate is viewers who click; conversion rate is clickers who buy.
