## How I use it

**Marketing, at the start of the product unit** (also fits Intro to Business before promotion). 25–35 minutes.

1. **Predict:** Before opening the tool, sketch the classic life cycle curve on the board with the four stage names and no labels on the axes. Ask: where is profit highest? Most students say maturity, where sales are highest. It's actually late growth.
2. **Test:** Pairs work through the four products. For each one:
   - **Name the stage** from the chart and the four facts. A wrong pick explains what that stage would look like.
   - **Try marketing moves.** Every move shows next year's sales as a dashed line. Students can try all three; encourage it, since the wrong moves teach as much as the right one.
   - **Plan production.** This is the operations side: how much to make and invest at that stage.
3. **Explain:** As each stage is named, the product lands on the big curve below. Finish with the Try it questions. #2 (why the same price cut is wrong for two different reasons) is the one to discuss as a class.

## Answer notes

| # | Product | Stage | Signs | Best marketing move | Best production call |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sleep-tracking smart ring | Introduction | 4,000 units, +33% from a tiny base, losing $180,000, 1 competitor | Explain the product (awareness) | Small batches, improve from feedback |
| 2 | Plant-based nuggets | Growth | 128,000 units, +178%, profit up to $520,000, competitors 2 → 9 | Wider distribution and brand preference | Scale up production |
| 3 | Sports drink | Maturity | 500,000 units, flat, profit slipping, 25 competitors | Differentiate (new flavors) and loyalty | Cut costs |
| 4 | DVD player | Decline | 100,000 units, −51%, profit down to $45,000, competitors leaving | Cut promotion, serve loyal buyers (harvest) | Sell off inventory, plan the exit |

What each move does to sales a year later (last quarter shown):

| Product | Best move | Other moves |
|---|---|---|
| Smart ring (now 4,000) | 15,000 | Half price: 6,000 (and each ring loses more). Comparison ads: 5,000 |
| Nuggets (now 128,000) | 262,000 | Big price hike: 120,000. Cut ads: 155,000 |
| Sports drink (now 500,000) | 530,000 | "What is a sports drink" ads: 498,000. 30% price cut: 505,000 after rivals match |
| DVD player (now 100,000) | 40,000, but still profitable | Big ad campaign: 76,000 at a loss. New features: 70,000 |

The DVD player is the tricky one: the best move has the *lowest* sales. Point out that the goal in decline is profit, not sales. The other two moves slow the drop a little and cost more than they bring in.

**Standard note:** MBA Research's current Marketing standards don't have a product life cycle indicator. VM:006 is in Management & Entrepreneurship and covers the operations side, which is why every product includes a production decision.

The data are made up for teaching. Real products move through the stages at very different speeds: a phone model can go from launch to decline in two years, while some snack brands have been mature for a century.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Profit is highest when sales are highest."** Profit usually peaks in late growth. By maturity, competitors are pushing prices and profits down even while sales are near their peak.
- **"Fast growth from a tiny number means growth stage."** The smart ring grew 33%, but it's 4,000 units with heavy losses and one competitor. Introduction can show big percentages from a small base.
- **"Lower prices always sell more."** Not when customers don't know the product (introduction) or when rivals match the cut (maturity).
- **"A falling product needs more advertising."** In decline, more ads usually cost more than they bring back. The smart move is often to spend less and earn what's left.
- **"Maturity means it's time to quit."** Mature products are often a company's most reliable profit. They pay for the next product's introduction stage.
- **Every product goes through every stage on schedule.** Some products fail in introduction. Some come back from decline (vinyl records). The curve is a pattern, not a law.
