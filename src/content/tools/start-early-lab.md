## How I use it

**Personal Finance, saving and investing unit.** 15–20 minutes.

1. **Predict:** Read the "Save 10 years, then stop" story out loud before showing the tool. Saver A puts away $100 a month from 18 to 28, then stops forever. Saver B starts at 28 and saves $100 a month until 65. Who has more at 65? Most students pick Saver B, who put in almost four times as much.
2. **Test:** Load the preset and reveal the answer. Then pairs work the "Try it" questions.
3. **Explain:** Each pair finishes this sentence with a number from the tool: "Starting early matters because..."

Point at the chart: Saver A's line keeps climbing after 28 with no new deposits. That's compound growth doing the work.

Be honest about the 7%. It's a common estimate for long-run stock market averages, not a promise, and real returns bounce around from year to year. The third preset shows the other side: money in a 0.5% savings account is safe but barely grows. This is a good lead-in to risk and diversification.

- The tool compounds monthly: each month the balance grows by the yearly return ÷ 12, then the deposit goes in. "Stops at 28" means the last deposit is made at age 27.
- Taxes, fees, and inflation are left out to keep the comparison clear.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Whoever puts in more money ends up with more."** Time can beat deposits. Saver A in the second preset put in $32,400 less and still won.
- **"I'll start saving when I earn real money."** Small amounts early beat bigger amounts later. $50 a month from 16 grows to more than $50 a month from 26 doubled.
- **"7% a year is guaranteed."** It's a long-run average. Some years are much better, and some lose money.
- **"Interest only grows on what I put in."** Compound interest grows on the interest too. By 65, most of Saver A's money is growth, not deposits.
