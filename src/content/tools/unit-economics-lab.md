## How I use it

**Right after Marketing Funnel**, which already shows students the cost to win one customer. This tool asks the next question: is that customer worth it? 20–30 minutes.

1. **Predict:** Show the three examples' names only. Which business can afford to spend the most to win a customer? Most students pick the snack box because it has the most customers. It's the one losing money.
2. **Test:** Load each example. Point at the chart: one customer starts in the red (what it cost to win them), climbs each month they keep buying, and goes flat when they leave. Where the line crosses zero is the payback point.
3. **Explain:** Pairs work the Try it questions. #1 is the big idea: when each customer loses money, growing faster makes it worse. #3 shows why keeping customers is often cheaper than finding new ones.

**Paper first (optional):** before opening the tool, have students work the food truck by hand from the four formulas in The math. The tool then checks their work and handles the "what if" questions that are tedious on paper.

## Answer notes

| Example | CAC | Profit per month | LTV | LTV to CAC | Payback | Per customer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Food truck loyalty app | $900 ÷ 30 = **$30** | $12 × 2 × 55% = $13.20 | × 6 = **$79.20** | **2.64** | 2.3 months | +$49.20 |
| Snack subscription box | $4,000 ÷ 100 = **$40** | $25 × 1 × 30% = $7.50 | × 4 = **$30** | **0.75** | Never (5.3 months, but they leave after 4) | −$10 |
| Online tutoring | $1,500 ÷ 10 = **$150** | $40 × 4 × 70% = $112 | × 5 = **$560** | **3.73** | 1.3 months | +$410 |

Try it answers:

1. Doubling the snack box budget to $8,000 for 200 customers keeps CAC at $40, so it doubles the loss: 200 × −$10 = −$2,000 a month instead of −$1,000.
2. 3 to 1 needs LTV of $120. At $7.50 a month that's 16 months, or keep 4 months and get CAC down to $10.
3. Food truck at 9 months: $13.20 × 9 = $118.80. $118.80 ÷ $30 = 3.96.
4. Tutoring's $112 a month pays back $150 in 1.34 months, about six weeks.

The ratio color: red below 1 to 1 (losing money on each customer), yellow from 1 to 3 (profitable but thin), green at 3 or more. 3 to 1 is a common rule of thumb, not a law. A business with very low other costs can live with less.

**Simplifications to mention if students ask.** LTV here uses gross profit, not revenue, which is the more honest version. Real businesses also lose some customers every month (churn) instead of everyone leaving at once; "months a customer stays" is the average. If 25% of customers leave each month, the average customer stays 1 ÷ 25% = 4 months.

## Predict first

Two prediction cards sit above the challenges. Students pick Goes up, Goes down, or Stays the same; the tool makes the change on screen and shows before and after.

- **Double the ads, 50% more customers** (snack box, $4,000 to $8,000 and 100 to 150 customers): worth-to-cost **goes down**, 0.75 to 0.56. Each customer now costs about $53 instead of $40. Follow-up: why does the next dollar of ads usually bring in fewer customers than the first? (The easiest customers are won first.)
- **Customers stay longer** (food truck app, 6 to 8 months): months to earn back a customer **stays the same**, about 2.3. Payback is cost to win ÷ profit per month, and neither changed. Follow-up: what did change? (Each customer is worth $105.60 instead of $79.20, so the ratio rises to 3.52.)

## Challenges

Three challenge cards sit under the tool. Each Start button loads its scenario and makes a link you can post. Sample solutions (there are others):

- **Hit 3 to 1** (food truck app, spend and new customers locked): keep customers 7 months or more (3.08), or raise the average order to $14 (3.08), or raise the margin to 63% (3.02). Ask which is easiest for a food truck.
- **Save the snack box** (box price, one a month, and spend locked): keep customers 7 months or more (+$12.50 at 7, +$20 at 8), or raise the margin to 50%, or win 200 new customers a month with the same spend. Debrief: which one would a subscription box try first?
- **Spend more, stay healthy** (tutoring, $2,000 spend, 10 new students, $40 sessions): students stay 6 months (3.36), or 5 sessions a month (3.5), or a 75% margin (3.0). If you spend more to win each customer, each one has to be worth more.

## Common misconceptions

- **"More customers always means more profit."** Only if each one is worth more than it cost. The snack box proves it.
- **Using revenue for lifetime value.** A customer who spends $100 doesn't bring in $100 of profit. The product costs money too, which is why margin is in the formula.
- **Forgetting that customers leave.** Payback only counts if it happens before the customer stops buying. The snack box would pay back in 5.3 months, but its customers leave after 4.
- **"Retention is free."** It isn't (punch cards, discounts, better service), but every extra month from a customer you already won adds profit with no new acquisition cost.
